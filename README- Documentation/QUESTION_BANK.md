# Question Bank by Topic ID

This file provides ready-to-use viva/demo/classroom prompts for each supported `topic_id`.

For each topic:
- **Ask**: sample questions you can pose to students.
- **Expected Answer**: key points a strong student answer should include.
- **Common Misconceptions**: likely incorrect ideas to watch for.
- **Follow-up Probe**: Socratic prompt to deepen understanding.

---

## `G6_S1_ORG_CHARS` — Living vs non-living / characteristics of life

### 1) Core Identification
- **Ask:** "How do we decide whether something is living or non-living?"
- **Expected Answer:** Living things show life processes such as nutrition, respiration, growth, response to stimuli, reproduction, and excretion. Non-living things do not perform these biologically.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "If it moves, it is alive"; "If it gets bigger, it is alive."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Can you name one non-living thing that moves and explain why it is still non-living?"

### 2) Borderline Example
- **Ask:** "A crystal becomes larger over time. Is it living?"
- **Expected Answer:** No. Crystal growth is physical deposition, not biological growth with metabolism/cells.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Any growth means life."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "What life process is missing in crystals?"

### 3) Plant Misconception
- **Ask:** "Plants do not walk. Are they non-living?"
- **Expected Answer:** Plants are living because they carry out life processes (photosynthesis/nutrition, growth, respiration, reproduction, response to light/touch).
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Only animals are living."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "How does a plant respond to its environment?"

## `G6_S1_ORG_CLASS` — Classification of organisms

### 1) Purpose of Classification
- **Ask:** "Why do scientists classify organisms?"
- **Expected Answer:** To group organisms by shared characteristics, make identification easier, and understand relationships.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Classification is only for naming."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "How does classification help when we discover a new organism?"

### 2) Vertebrate vs Invertebrate
- **Ask:** "What is the main difference between vertebrates and invertebrates?"
- **Expected Answer:** Vertebrates have a backbone; invertebrates do not.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Big animals are vertebrates, small ones are invertebrates."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Give one example each and justify your choice."

### 3) Habitat vs Group
- **Ask:** "Do all organisms living in water belong to the same classification group?"
- **Expected Answer:** No. Habitat does not define classification alone; body structure and biological characteristics matter.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Same habitat means same class."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Compare a fish and a dolphin: same habitat, different classification?"

## `G6_S2_MAT_PROPS` — Physical properties of materials

### 1) Property-Based Selection
- **Ask:** "Why is steel used for bridges while rubber is used for tires?"
- **Expected Answer:** Material choice depends on properties. Steel: high strength/hardness; rubber: elasticity/grip/flexibility.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "One material can suit all purposes."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Which property is most important for a helmet shell and why?"

### 2) Hardness vs Strength
- **Ask:** "Are hardness and strength the same thing?"
- **Expected Answer:** No. Hardness is resistance to scratching/indentation; strength is resistance to breaking under load.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Hard materials are always strongest."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Can you name a hard but brittle material?"

### 3) Conductivity Context
- **Ask:** "Why are cooking pots often metal but handles are plastic or wood?"
- **Expected Answer:** Metals conduct heat well (useful for cooking); plastic/wood are poor heat conductors (safer to hold).
- **Common Misconceptions:** "All parts should be metal for better cooking."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "What might happen if the handle were also metal with no insulation?"

## `G6_S2_MAT_STATES` — States of matter

### 1) State Characteristics
- **Ask:** "How are solids, liquids, and gases different?"
- **Expected Answer:** Solids have fixed shape and volume; liquids have fixed volume but no fixed shape; gases have neither fixed shape nor fixed volume.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Liquids have no volume"; "Gases have no mass."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Can you give one daily-life example for each state?"

### 2) Melting vs Dissolving
- **Ask:** "Is melting ice the same as dissolving sugar in water?"
- **Expected Answer:** No. Melting is a state change (solid to liquid) of one substance; dissolving is mixing a solute into a solvent.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Anything that disappears in water has melted."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "How could you recover dissolved salt from saltwater?"

### 3) Evaporation vs Boiling
- **Ask:** "What is the difference between evaporation and boiling?"
- **Expected Answer:** Evaporation happens at the surface and can occur below boiling point; boiling occurs throughout the liquid at a specific temperature (for given pressure).
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Evaporation only happens at 100 C."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Why do wet clothes dry even when water is not boiling?"

## `G6_S4_ENE_SOURCES` — Energy sources and forms

### 1) Renewable vs Non-renewable
- **Ask:** "What is the difference between renewable and non-renewable energy sources?"
- **Expected Answer:** Renewable sources replenish naturally on short timescales (solar, wind, hydro); non-renewable sources are finite and take very long to form (coal, oil, gas).
- **Common Misconceptions:** "If a source is natural, it is automatically renewable."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Where would you place biomass, and what condition matters?"

### 2) Source vs Form
- **Ask:** "Is electricity an energy source or an energy form?"
- **Expected Answer:** Electricity is an energy form/carrier, usually generated from sources (renewable or non-renewable).
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Electricity itself is a primary natural source."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Name two different sources that can generate electricity."

### 3) Conservation Perspective
- **Ask:** "Why should we conserve non-renewable energy resources?"
- **Expected Answer:** They are limited, extraction/use can harm the environment, and overuse threatens long-term availability.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Technology can instantly replace all non-renewables."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "What is one realistic conservation action at home or school?"

## `G6_S8_ELE_CIRCUITS` — Simple electric circuits

### 1) Complete Path
- **Ask:** "Why does a bulb light only when the circuit is complete?"
- **Expected Answer:** Electric current needs a closed loop from the source through components and back to the source.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "One wire from battery to bulb is enough."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "What exact break in the path would stop the bulb from lighting?"

### 2) Series vs Parallel Basics
- **Ask:** "What happens to bulbs in a series circuit if one bulb fails?"
- **Expected Answer:** In series, one failure opens the path and all bulbs go off; in parallel, other branches can still work.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Series and parallel behave the same when one bulb fails."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Which setup is better for house wiring, and why?"

### 3) Battery Polarity
- **Ask:** "Why do we connect battery terminals correctly?"
- **Expected Answer:** Correct polarity ensures intended current direction and proper operation of components.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Polarity never matters."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Which classroom components might still work either way, and which might not?"

## `G6_S8_ELE_CONDINS` — Conductors vs insulators

### 1) Material Behavior
- **Ask:** "What is the difference between an electrical conductor and an insulator?"
- **Expected Answer:** Conductors allow electric current to pass easily (e.g., copper, aluminum); insulators resist current flow (e.g., rubber, plastic, glass).
- **Common Misconceptions:** "All solids conduct electricity."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Why is copper inside a wire but plastic outside?"

### 2) Metal Overgeneralization
- **Ask:** "Are all metals equally good conductors?"
- **Expected Answer:** No. Many metals conduct, but conductivity differs by material.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Any metal works the same in circuits."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "Why might copper be preferred over iron for wires?"

### 3) Safety Context
- **Ask:** "Why should you avoid touching electrical devices with wet hands?"
- **Expected Answer:** Water with dissolved ions can increase conductivity, raising shock risk.
- **Common Misconceptions:** "Pure water always conducts strongly" or "Water never conducts."
- **Follow-up Probe:** "What role does dissolved salt/minerals play?"

## Quick Use Template (for any topic)

- **Diagnostic question:** "What is your rule for deciding this?"
- **Confidence check:** "How sure are you (0-100%) and why?"
- **Counterexample prompt:** "Can you think of an example that challenges your first idea?"
- **Transfer prompt:** "Where would this concept matter in real life?"
